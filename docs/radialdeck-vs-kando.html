Comparison

RadialDeck and Kando.

Both put a pie menu under your cursor. Kando is a free, open-source pie menu for Windows, macOS and Linux. RadialDeck is a Windows-only radial menu built around the program you are in, the files you selected and the text you copied. Here is how they differ, so you can pick the one that fits.

KandoRadialDeck
Runs onWindows, macOS and LinuxWindows 10 (1809 or later) and Windows 11, as a native Windows app
PriceFree and open source; the author accepts donationsFree for two rings. Pro is 24 € a year, VAT included, with a 14-day trial and a 30-day refund
Source codeOpen source on GitHubClosed source. No account and no telemetry
Opens withA shortcut (Ctrl+Space for the example menu); mouse, stylus, touch, controller or keyboardA hotkey held or tapped, a side or middle mouse button, or holding the right button; arrow keys, type-to-find and number keys once it is open
What a slice doesLaunch applications, simulate keyboard shortcuts, open files and moreLaunch or switch to programs, send shortcuts, paste snippets, run PowerShell or cmd with the selection or clipboard, 32 clipboard text transforms, window switcher, clipboard history, scroll to adjust, copy text from the screen, macros
Out of the boxAn example menu to start fromA gallery of 1,180 ready-made actions for 86 programs, and packs for the programs it finds installed
Windows installerCode-signed through the SignPath FoundationNot code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen asks once before the first run

Kando's column comes from its public README and documentation as of 30 September 2026. If something here is out of date, tell us and we will fix it.

Pick Kando if

you also work on a Mac or on Linux and want the same menu everywhere, you want open-source software, or you drive your computer with a pen, a touch screen or a game controller.

Pick RadialDeck if

you spend the day in Windows programs like Excel, Outlook, Explorer, AutoCAD or a terminal, and want a ring that changes with the program in front, acts on the files you selected and fixes the text you copied, with most of it ready-made. See the programs it knows.
