Privacy policy

Last updated 2 October 2026.

Who we are

RadialDeck is made and sold by Stefan Kasamakov, Sofia, Bulgaria, who is the controller of the personal data described below. Contact: hello@radialdeck.com.

What RadialDeck collects

Nothing about you. The application has no telemetry, no crash reporting, no analytics and no accounts.

It makes two kinds of network request. The first is an optional update check: every few hours it asks GitHub Releases (api.github.com) for the latest version number and, if you click "install", downloads the installer from GitHub. The request carries no identifier other than the application name and version. GitHub's own privacy statement applies to that request. You can turn the check off in Settings ("Check for updates"); the app then makes no network requests at all.

The second happens only if you have entered a subscription key: once a day the application asks radialdeck.com about that key, sending the key’s random id and nothing else (no name, no e-mail, no information about your computer). The answer is today’s date, a renewed key when your subscription has renewed, and whether the payment was refunded. If radialdeck.com cannot be reached, the key keeps working offline for up to 60 days (or 720 hours of use), after which Pro waits for the next successful check. A perpetual key, the free version and the trial never make this request.

What stays on your computer

  • %APPDATA%\RadialDeck\config.json: your rings and actions. Yours to edit, export or delete.
  • %APPDATA%\RadialDeck\license.json: your licence key and the date of your first run (for the trial). Verified locally.
  • %APPDATA%\RadialDeck\debug.log: lifecycle events and errors. It never contains keystrokes or clipboard contents.

The clipboard history ring

One optional slice type opens a ring of the last few things you copied, so you can paste an earlier one. It is off unless you add that slice to a ring yourself, and only then does RadialDeck ask Windows to tell it when the clipboard changes.

Those entries live in the application's memory and nowhere else. They are never written to config.json, never written to the log, never sent anywhere, and they are gone the moment RadialDeck closes or you remove the slice. What you copy while the feature is off is not seen at all.

What your recipes may do

A recipe is an action you chose: opening a URL, running a command, pasting text. Those actions behave like anything else you run on your PC; for example "Ask ChatGPT about the copied text" opens chatgpt.com with that text in your browser. RadialDeck itself does not transmit it.

Reporting a problem

"Report a problem" in the tray menu opens an e-mail in your own mail program and shows you debug.log. Nothing is sent unless you send that e-mail yourself, and you can read the log before attaching it.

The website

radialdeck.com counts visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which sets no cookies and stores nothing in your browser. It records which page was opened, the site you came from, your country and the kind of browser and device, and shows them to us only as totals. Your IP address is not kept and you are not followed from one site to another.
