Outlook Quick Steps: useful examples, their limits, and a faster way to reply

Quick Steps bundle several Outlook actions into one click. Practical examples to set up today, what changes in the new Outlook, where Quick Steps stop, and how a ring under the cursor covers the rest.

If you handle a lot of email, Quick Steps are the most underused feature in Outlook. A Quick Step applies several actions to a message at once: move it, mark it as read, flag it, forward it, reply and delete. One click instead of four.

This guide gives practical examples, explains what is different in the new Outlook, and covers what Quick Steps cannot do.

Where Quick Steps live

In both classic Outlook for Windows and the new Outlook, Quick Steps are on the Home tab of the ribbon. Select a message, then click a Quick Step. You create and edit them from the same place.

Classic Outlook comes with a few ready-made ones, as Microsoft describes them:

  • Move to: moves the selected message to a folder you choose and marks it as read.
  • To Manager: forwards the message to your manager.
  • Team Email: forwards the message to others in your team.
  • Done: moves the message to a folder, marks it complete and marks it as read.
  • Reply & Delete: opens a reply and deletes the original message.

One warning from Microsoft's documentation: Quick Steps cannot be undone with Ctrl+Z or a menu command, because some actions, such as deleting, may not be reversible. Test a new Quick Step on a message you do not need.

Examples worth setting up

These are combinations that save real clicks. Build them with New Quick Step and pick the actions in order.

1. "Handled"

Mark as read, remove the flag, move to an "Archive" or "Done" folder. One click clears a message out of the inbox once you have dealt with it.

2. "Waiting for reply"

Flag for follow-up, categorise as "Waiting", move to a "Waiting" folder. When you send a question and need to chase it later, this keeps it visible without cluttering the inbox.

3. "To accounts"

Forward to the accounts team's address, mark as read, move to "Invoices". Useful for anyone who receives invoices and passes them on.

4. "Meeting from this email"

Use the Reply with Meeting action to turn the message into a meeting invitation with the same people. Handy when an email thread needs a call.

5. "Reply and file" (classic Outlook)

Reply, then move the original to a project folder. You answer and file in one go.

The new Outlook offers a shorter list of actions than classic Outlook, and Quick Steps made in classic Outlook do not carry over by themselves. If an action above is missing, build the closest version from what the new Outlook offers.

Keyboard shortcuts for Quick Steps

Each Quick Step can get a keyboard shortcut of the form Ctrl+Shift plus a number.

  • In classic Outlook, you can choose Ctrl+Shift+1 to Ctrl+Shift+9.
  • In the new Outlook and Outlook on the web, only Ctrl+Shift+5 to Ctrl+Shift+9 are available, because the lower numbers are used for other commands.

So in the new Outlook you have five shortcut slots at most, and you have to remember which number does what.

Where Quick Steps stop

Quick Steps are good at moving and marking messages. They are less good at:

  • Writing. A Quick Step can open a reply, but there is no built-in action that inserts a prepared text into it. For standard answers you need My Templates in the new Outlook, or Quick Parts in classic Outlook, which are separate features with their own steps.
  • Working outside Outlook. A Quick Step cannot copy the sender's address into Excel, open the attachment folder, or start a Teams chat.
  • Being remembered. Ten Quick Steps in a ribbon gallery, with five keyboard slots, is a lot to keep in your head.

Use both

Quick Steps and a ring work well together. Give your two or three most-used Quick Steps a shortcut (Ctrl+Shift+5 and up in the new Outlook), and put those shortcuts into slices with the Send keys action. Then "Handled" and "Waiting for reply" sit in the ring next to Reply and the template, with no numbers to remember.

Templates without Pro

If you are on the free version, a Paste slice holds any text and pastes it where the cursor is. Press Ctrl+R to reply, then flick to the slice. Two steps instead of one, but no template menus.
